### Grötzsch's theorem, Ore's conjecture for k = 4, and the Grünbaum–Aksenov theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000005](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000005&version=2)
(version 2; version 1 contained the first two results)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--grotzsch-ore--8ebd0df3e665](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--grotzsch-ore--8ebd0df3e665/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000005-v2/ff6d5123c06e4c99bb6074e0a72e8be8d595eefa).

Every finite triangle-free planar graph is 3-colourable (Grötzsch, 1959), deduced as in
Kostochka–Yancey (2014) from their proof of the case k = 4 of Ore's conjecture: every 4-critical
graph satisfies 3|E| ≥ 5|V| − 2, by the potential method. Planarity is defined combinatorially
(genus-zero rotation systems), and the topological crossing argument of the paper is replaced by
a K₅-minor argument with an apex inside a face. Sanity checks: K₄ is planar, K₅ and K₃,₃ are not.
Version 2 adds the Grünbaum–Aksenov theorem (every planar graph with at most three triangles is
3-colourable), following Borodin–Kostochka–Lidický–Yancey, together with their precolouring
extension theorems for 4- and 5-cycles and a combinatorial Jordan-curve (region) theorem for
rotation systems.

### The Hajnal–Szemerédi theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000007](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000007&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--hajnal-szemeredi--c11276c44bab](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--hajnal-szemeredi--c11276c44bab/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000007-v1/ba34061e3cf12dfc8297610d5d7b3f0afac7ec54).

Every finite graph of maximum degree at most r has an equitable (r + 1)-colouring: the colour
classes differ in size by at most one (Hajnal–Szemerédi, 1970), following the short proof of
Kierstead and Kostochka (2008). Also the exact form: if |V| = s(r + 1), there is a proper
(r + 1)-colouring whose classes all have exactly s vertices.

### The list chromatic index of complete graphs (Häggkvist–Janssen)

[PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000003](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000003&version=1)

### Vizing's theorem and equitable edge colourings

[PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000004](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000004&version=2)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--vizing--01fa5f875100](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--vizing--01fa5f875100/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000004-v2/33b2de836095cf92bc1dec33b6f8787e4036bbc6).

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of Vizing's theorem: every finite simple graph G has a proper edge colouring with Δ(G) + 1 colours, stated with Mathlib's line graph (G.lineGraph is (Δ(G) + 1)-colourable, hence χ'(G) ≤ Δ(G) + 1), proved with fans and Kempe chain exchanges. It also proves the equitable refinement: for every c ≥ Δ(G) + 1 there is a proper edge colouring with colours Fin c in which any two colour classes (including unused colours) differ in size by at most one (McDiarmid 1972), obtained by balancing a Vizing colouring with alternating-component moves that strictly decrease a square energy. Version 2 adds the lower bound Δ(G) ≤ χ'(G) (the edges at a vertex of maximum degree form a clique of the line graph) and hence the dichotomy χ'(G) ∈ {Δ(G), Δ(G) + 1} (class 1 / class 2).

### Wagner's planarity criterion and list colouring

[PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000005](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000005&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--planar-list-colouring--03a15f1929ab](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--planar-list-colouring--03a15f1929ab/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000005-v1/ec1eafbc091713857eb5683a46e460902b2d47b3).

A finite graph is planar if and only if it has neither K₅ nor K₃,₃ as a minor (Wagner, 1937).
Every K₅-minor-free graph is 5-choosable (Škrekovski) and DP-5-colourable (Li–Wang); hence planar
graphs are 5-choosable (Thomassen) and DP-5-colourable (Dvořák–Postle). Some planar graph is not
4-choosable (Voigt). The Four Colour Theorem is equivalent to Hadwiger's conjecture for K₅
(Wagner), neither side being proved.

### Near-regular hypergraph matchings and the asymptotic triangle-packing gap

[PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000011](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000011&version=2)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--asymptotic-triangle-packing--a2c3300bc8ff](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--asymptotic-triangle-packing--a2c3300bc8ff/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000011-v2/760e503972aad8ca9da7904ce72e5cce405f5991).

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of four results. (1) A finite nibble theorem: r-uniform hypergraphs whose degrees are at most (1+μ)d everywhere and at least (1−μ)d outside an exceptional set of at most η|V| vertices, with codegrees at most μd, have matchings with at least (1−β)|V|/r edges. (2) The fractional–integral triangle-packing gap is o(n²) uniformly over finite graphs: every large graph has an edge-disjoint triangle family P such that every fractional triangle packing has weight at most |P| + εn² (the triangle case of Haxell–Rödl). (3) The cover form: a fractional triangle cover of weight at most |P| + εn² exists. (4) Version 2, the transfer form used in decomposition theory: every large graph on n vertices that has a fractional triangle decomposition has an edge-disjoint triangle family covering all but at most ηn² edges; it follows from (2), since a fractional decomposition is a fractional packing of weight e(G)/3 and a packing P covers exactly 3|P| edges. The proof of (1)–(3) combines a nibble with second-moment and Freedman-type concentration, a low-degree core reduction, Mathlib's Szemerédi regularity lemma with a cluster linear program rounded by explicit designs, and LP duality.

### Kuratowski's theorem

A finite graph is planar if and only if it contains no subdivision of K₅ and no subdivision of

### Mader's theorem and Hadwiger's conjecture for t ≤ 4

### Brooks' theorem, degree-choosability and blocks

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000003](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000003&version=2)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--brooks--c255f4f55cbf](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--brooks--c255f4f55cbf/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000003-v2/b99e6f500256586626396de9c2d6fd4d96119d60).

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of Brooks' theorem: for k ≥ 3, a finite graph of maximum degree at most k without a (k+1)-clique is k-colourable (no connectedness assumption), and a finite connected graph that is neither complete nor an odd cycle is colourable with as many colours as its maximum degree. The proof uses greedy colourings in Lovász's style and an induction on the number of edges with a separation-and-gluing step. Version 2 adds the Erdős–Rubin–Taylor / Borodin theorem (a finite connected graph is degree-choosable, i.e. colourable from any lists with |L(v)| ≥ deg(v), if and only if it is not a Gallai tree: some block is neither complete nor an odd cycle), Brooks' theorem for lists (Vizing 1976), and a library of blocks of graphs (blocks as maximal nonseparable vertex sets, cut vertices, unique block per edge, blocks meeting in at most one vertex, the block–cut tree of a connected graph, and the agreement of the block and induced-subgraph descriptions of Gallai trees).

### Berge's theorem, the Tutte–Berge formula and the Gallai–Edmonds theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000011](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000011&version=2)

### The Gallai–Hasse–Roy–Vitaver theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000015](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000015&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--gallai-roy--242cd45a936c](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--gallai-roy--242cd45a936c/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000015-v1/e737218bf80c3d3684d670f5f8aa9a8257e1a52f).

If an orientation of a finite graph has no directed path on more than k vertices, the graph is
k-colourable; conversely, every k-colourable graph has such an orientation. Hence the chromatic
number is the minimum, over all orientations, of the number of vertices of a longest directed path.

### Euler's theorem on Eulerian circuits and trails, and Petersen's 2-factor theorem

### Graphic sequences: the Erdős–Gallai and Havel–Hakimi theorems

### Fractional triangle decompositions of dense tripartite graphs

[PALOMAR-2026-10-30-000008](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-30-000008&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--fractional-triangle-decompositions-tripartite--1b438fcdd323](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--fractional-triangle-decompositions-tripartite--1b438fcdd323/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-30-000008-v1/d2331132eac8b9c9c1891ba14162745d567ebfdb).

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of the main results of Allsop, Bowtell, Lesgourgues and Petrova, "Improved bounds on completion of partial Latin squares" (arXiv:2609.37877, 2026): there is η > 0 (here η = 1/20000) such that every K₃-divisible spanning subgraph of K_{n,n,n} with minimum partite degree at least (0.769 − η)n has a fractional triangle decomposition (Theorem 1.3). Also formalized: the Farkas characterization (Lemma 2.1); the reduction to triangle-covered, balanced, untied and nonzero edge weightings (Proposition 3.1, Corollary 3.5), with Lemma 3.4 proved through an integral maximum flow; the non-uniform proportional discharging (Definition 4.1) and its two estimates (Propositions 4.2 and 4.3); and the numerical inequality g_d ≥ 1 for the article's piecewise-linear f (Proposition 4.4). The two-variable inequalities of Proposition 4.4, which the article settles by numerical minimisation, are proved exactly by Bernstein certificates checked in Lean. Not formalized: the article's Corollary 1.4 (completion of partial Latin squares), which also needs the fractional-to-exact transfer of Barber, Kühn, Lo, Osthus and Taylor for tripartite graphs.
