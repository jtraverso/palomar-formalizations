### Grötzsch's theorem, Ore's conjecture for k = 4, and the Grünbaum–Aksenov theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000005](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000005&version=2)
(version 2; version 1 contained the first two results)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--grotzsch-ore--8ebd0df3e665](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--grotzsch-ore--8ebd0df3e665/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000005-v2/ff6d5123c06e4c99bb6074e0a72e8be8d595eefa).

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--grotzsch-ore--8ebd0df3e665](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--grotzsch-ore--8ebd0df3e665/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000005-v2/ff6d5123c06e4c99bb6074e0a72e8be8d595eefa).

Every finite triangle-free planar graph is 3-colourable (Grötzsch, 1959), deduced as in
Kostochka–Yancey (2014) from their proof of the case k = 4 of Ore's conjecture: every 4-critical
graph satisfies 3|E| ≥ 5|V| − 2, by the potential method. Planarity is defined combinatorially
(genus-zero rotation systems), and the topological crossing argument of the paper is replaced by
a K₅-minor argument with an apex inside a face. Sanity checks: K₄ is planar, K₅ and K₃,₃ are not.
Version 2 adds the Grünbaum–Aksenov theorem (every planar graph with at most three triangles is
3-colourable), following Borodin–Kostochka–Lidický–Yancey, together with their precolouring
extension theorems for 4- and 5-cycles and a combinatorial Jordan-curve (region) theorem for
rotation systems.

### The Hajnal–Szemerédi theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000007](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000007&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--hajnal-szemeredi--c11276c44bab](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--hajnal-szemeredi--c11276c44bab/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000007-v1/ba34061e3cf12dfc8297610d5d7b3f0afac7ec54).

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--hajnal-szemeredi--c11276c44bab](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--hajnal-szemeredi--c11276c44bab/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000007-v1/ba34061e3cf12dfc8297610d5d7b3f0afac7ec54).

Every finite graph of maximum degree at most r has an equitable (r + 1)-colouring: the colour
classes differ in size by at most one (Hajnal–Szemerédi, 1970), following the short proof of
Kierstead and Kostochka (2008). Also the exact form: if |V| = s(r + 1), there is a proper
(r + 1)-colouring whose classes all have exactly s vertices.

### The list chromatic index of complete graphs (Häggkvist–Janssen)

[PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000003](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000003&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--haggkvist-janssen--3449623606b5](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--haggkvist-janssen--3449623606b5/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000003-v1/807e08bd7c480d4fae80c1b78377d23989d39ab0).

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--haggkvist-janssen--3449623606b5](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--haggkvist-janssen--3449623606b5/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000003-v1/807e08bd7c480d4fae80c1b78377d23989d39ab0).

The complete graph Kₙ can be edge-coloured from lists of size n assigned to its edges
(Häggkvist–Janssen, 1997): its list chromatic index is at most n. The proof goes through the
Alon–Tarsi polynomial method with a certificate built from permutations.

### Vizing's theorem and equitable edge colourings

[PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000004](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000004&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--vizing--01fa5f875100](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--vizing--01fa5f875100/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000004-v1/01c72ed7efde2353a4bb1654f69b5c0bc5038863).

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--vizing--01fa5f875100](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--vizing--01fa5f875100/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000004-v1/01c72ed7efde2353a4bb1654f69b5c0bc5038863).

Every finite simple graph has a proper edge colouring with Δ + 1 colours (Vizing, 1964), proved
with fans and Kempe chains; moreover, for every c ≥ Δ + 1 there is a proper edge colouring with
c colours whose colour classes differ in size by at most one (McDiarmid, 1972).

### Wagner's planarity criterion and list colouring

[PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000005](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000005&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--planar-list-colouring--03a15f1929ab](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--planar-list-colouring--03a15f1929ab/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000005-v1/ec1eafbc091713857eb5683a46e460902b2d47b3).

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--planar-list-colouring--03a15f1929ab](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--planar-list-colouring--03a15f1929ab/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000005-v1/ec1eafbc091713857eb5683a46e460902b2d47b3).

A finite graph is planar if and only if it has neither K₅ nor K₃,₃ as a minor (Wagner, 1937).
Every K₅-minor-free graph is 5-choosable (Škrekovski) and DP-5-colourable (Li–Wang); hence planar
graphs are 5-choosable (Thomassen) and DP-5-colourable (Dvořák–Postle). Some planar graph is not
4-choosable (Voigt). The Four Colour Theorem is equivalent to Hadwiger's conjecture for K₅
(Wagner), neither side being proved.

### Dross's theorem on fractional triangle decompositions

[PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000008](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000008&version=1)

decomposition (Dross, 2016), proved through a finite max-flow/min-cut theorem.

### Near-regular hypergraph matchings and the asymptotic triangle-packing gap

[PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000011](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000011&version=1)

### Kuratowski's theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000018](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000018&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--kuratowski--c5380f41889a](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--kuratowski--c5380f41889a/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000018-v1/696ec897a9b91933037767d897670aa30ba3683a).

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--kuratowski--c5380f41889a](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--kuratowski--c5380f41889a/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000018-v1/696ec897a9b91933037767d897670aa30ba3683a).

A finite graph is planar if and only if it contains no subdivision of K₅ and no subdivision of
K₃,₃ (Kuratowski, 1930), derived from Wagner's minor characterization: a subdivision of H gives H
as a minor, a K₃,₃ minor gives a K₃,₃ subdivision, and a K₅ minor gives a K₅ or a K₃,₃
subdivision.

### Kőnig's theorems and Dilworth's theorem

### Mader's theorem and Hadwiger's conjecture for t ≤ 4

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000001](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000001&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--hadwiger-mader--a4b96dfb7362](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--hadwiger-mader--a4b96dfb7362/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000001-v1/cb10154cd439a419d0bbf34052839dd27242b619).

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--hadwiger-mader--a4b96dfb7362](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--hadwiger-mader--a4b96dfb7362/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000001-v1/cb10154cd439a419d0bbf34052839dd27242b619).

A nonempty graph of average degree at least 2^(r−2) has a K_r minor (Mader, 1967); a graph without
a K₄ minor has a vertex of degree at most two and at most 2n − 3 edges (Dirac, 1952); graphs without
a K₃ minor are 2-colourable and graphs without a K₄ minor are 3-colourable (Hadwiger's conjecture
for t ≤ 4).

### Brooks' theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000003](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000003&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--brooks--c255f4f55cbf](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--brooks--c255f4f55cbf/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000003-v1/e82569027fe8582c647e297d7712af86af372b47).

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--brooks--c255f4f55cbf](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--brooks--c255f4f55cbf/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000003-v1/e82569027fe8582c647e297d7712af86af372b47).

For k ≥ 3, a finite graph of maximum degree at most k without a (k+1)-clique is k-colourable;

### Petersen's theorem

### Berge's theorem and the Tutte–Berge formula

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000011](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000011&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--berge-matching--b269b121c46f](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--berge-matching--b269b121c46f/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000011-v1/b9c91bfbbe08ee7266ee3cb3543dff4e4ca8bc8c).

A matching is maximum if and only if there is no augmenting path (Berge, 1957); and the maximum
size of a matching is the minimum over vertex sets U of (|V| + |U| − odd(G − U)) / 2 (Tutte–Berge
formula), derived from Mathlib's Tutte theorem.

### The Gallai–Hasse–Roy–Vitaver theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000015](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000015&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--gallai-roy--242cd45a936c](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--gallai-roy--242cd45a936c/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000015-v1/e737218bf80c3d3684d670f5f8aa9a8257e1a52f).

If an orientation of a finite graph has no directed path on more than k vertices, the graph is
k-colourable; conversely, every k-colourable graph has such an orientation. Hence the chromatic
number is the minimum, over all orientations, of the number of vertices of a longest directed path.

### Euler's theorem on Eulerian circuits and trails, and Petersen's 2-factor theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000016](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000016&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--euler-petersen--780912a29df2](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--euler-petersen--780912a29df2/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000016-v1/02c3f1a5327c5f066938860bb07e9fce226b36ca).

A finite connected graph has an Eulerian circuit iff every degree is even (starting at any vertex),
and an Eulerian trail iff it has 0 or 2 odd vertices (from a to b when those are a and b); every
even graph has an Eulerian orientation; and every finite 2k-regular graph is the edge-disjoint
union of k 2-factors (Petersen, 1891), proved via closed-trail decompositions and Hall's theorem.
Mathlib had the necessity directions of the Euler theorems; their sufficiency was a listed TODO.

### Graphic sequences: the Erdős–Gallai and Havel–Hakimi theorems

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000017](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000017&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--degree-sequences--21c02be05315](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--degree-sequences--21c02be05315/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000017-v1/b49808e0c0aade945472b777047148399a582858).

A non-increasing sequence of natural numbers is the degree sequence of a finite simple graph if and
only if its sum is even and every initial segment satisfies the Erdős–Gallai inequality (Erdős–Gallai,
1960); equivalently, if and only if its first term is at most the number of remaining terms, the next
d₀ terms are positive, and the Havel–Hakimi reduced sequence is graphic (Havel 1955, Hakimi 1962).

### The Cauchy–Binet formula, Kirchhoff's matrix-tree theorem and Cayley's formula

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000019](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000019&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--matrix-tree--5efe2b63de2b](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--matrix-tree--5efe2b63de2b/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000019-v1/aeab0f267c523dd3cac9a287b589f65a99d20d01).

The Cauchy–Binet formula for determinants of products of rectangular matrices over commutative
rings (not in Mathlib); Kirchhoff's matrix-tree theorem: the number of spanning trees of a finite

### Füredi's stability theorem and the Erdős–Simonovits stability theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000024](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000024&version=1)

Füredi's sharp stability theorem for cliques (a K_{p+1}-free graph with t(n,p) − t edges has a

### Pseudorandom hypergraph matchings (Ehard–Glock–Joos)

[PALOMAR-2026-10-17-000004](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-17-000004&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--pseudorandom-hypergraph-matchings--40c1ca802506](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--pseudorandom-hypergraph-matchings--40c1ca802506/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-17-000004-v1/3957f3c0dbc34fd310cb5fecf7941fddc9ef6c8f).

The main results of Ehard, Glock and Joos (CPC 2020): Pippenger's theorem (Theorem 1.1); in any
r-uniform hypergraph with Δ(H) ≤ Δ, codegrees ≤ Δ^{1−δ} and at most exp(Δ^{ε²}) edges, a matching M
with ω(M) = (1 ± Δ^{−ε}) ω(E(H))/Δ for up to exp(Δ^{ε²}) weight functions at once (Theorem 1.2) and
for weights on ℓ-tuples of edges (Theorem 1.3); the concentration inequality for pattern weights
(Theorem 3.1); and pseudorandom partial Steiner systems (Theorem 4.1). Self-contained over Mathlib:
Freedman and Bernstein are proved inside, and the paper's Molloy–Reed black box is replaced by a nibble.
