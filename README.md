### Grötzsch's theorem, Ore's conjecture for k = 4, and the Grünbaum–Aksenov theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000005](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000005&version=2)
(version 2; version 1 contained the first two results)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--grotzsch-ore--8ebd0df3e665](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--grotzsch-ore--8ebd0df3e665/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000005-v2/ff6d5123c06e4c99bb6074e0a72e8be8d595eefa).

Every finite triangle-free planar graph is 3-colourable (Grötzsch, 1959), deduced as in
Kostochka–Yancey (2014) from their proof of the case k = 4 of Ore's conjecture: every 4-critical
graph satisfies 3|E| ≥ 5|V| − 2, by the potential method. Planarity is defined combinatorially
(genus-zero rotation systems), and the topological crossing argument of the paper is replaced by
a K₅-minor argument with an apex inside a face. Sanity checks: K₄ is planar, K₅ and K₃,₃ are not.
Version 2 adds the Grünbaum–Aksenov theorem (every planar graph with at most three triangles is
3-colourable), following Borodin–Kostochka–Lidický–Yancey, together with their precolouring
extension theorems for 4- and 5-cycles and a combinatorial Jordan-curve (region) theorem for
rotation systems.

### The Hajnal–Szemerédi theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000007](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000007&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--hajnal-szemeredi--c11276c44bab](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--hajnal-szemeredi--c11276c44bab/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000007-v1/ba34061e3cf12dfc8297610d5d7b3f0afac7ec54).

Every finite graph of maximum degree at most r has an equitable (r + 1)-colouring: the colour
classes differ in size by at most one (Hajnal–Szemerédi, 1970), following the short proof of
Kierstead and Kostochka (2008). Also the exact form: if |V| = s(r + 1), there is a proper
(r + 1)-colouring whose classes all have exactly s vertices.

### The list chromatic index of complete graphs (Häggkvist–Janssen)

[PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000003](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000003&version=1)

### Vizing's theorem and equitable edge colourings

[PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000004](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000004&version=1)

### Wagner's planarity criterion and list colouring

[PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000005](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000005&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--planar-list-colouring--03a15f1929ab](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--planar-list-colouring--03a15f1929ab/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-15-000005-v1/ec1eafbc091713857eb5683a46e460902b2d47b3).

A finite graph is planar if and only if it has neither K₅ nor K₃,₃ as a minor (Wagner, 1937).
Every K₅-minor-free graph is 5-choosable (Škrekovski) and DP-5-colourable (Li–Wang); hence planar
graphs are 5-choosable (Thomassen) and DP-5-colourable (Dvořák–Postle). Some planar graph is not
4-choosable (Voigt). The Four Colour Theorem is equivalent to Hadwiger's conjecture for K₅
(Wagner), neither side being proved.

### Kuratowski's theorem

### Mader's theorem and Hadwiger's conjecture for t ≤ 4

### Berge's theorem and the Tutte–Berge formula

### The Gallai–Hasse–Roy–Vitaver theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000015](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000015&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--gallai-roy--242cd45a936c](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--gallai-roy--242cd45a936c/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000015-v1/e737218bf80c3d3684d670f5f8aa9a8257e1a52f).

If an orientation of a finite graph has no directed path on more than k vertices, the graph is
k-colourable; conversely, every k-colourable graph has such an orientation. Hence the chromatic
number is the minimum, over all orientations, of the number of vertices of a longest directed path.

### Euler's theorem on Eulerian circuits and trails, and Petersen's 2-factor theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000016](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000016&version=1)

A finite connected graph has an Eulerian circuit iff every degree is even (starting at any vertex),
and an Eulerian trail iff it has 0 or 2 odd vertices (from a to b when those are a and b); every

### Graphic sequences: the Erdős–Gallai and Havel–Hakimi theorems

[PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000017](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-16-000017&version=1)

A non-increasing sequence of natural numbers is the degree sequence of a finite simple graph if and

### The Nash-Williams–Tutte tree packing theorem and Nash-Williams' arboricity theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-17-000008](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-17-000008&version=1)

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of three classical results on edge-disjoint trees and forests in finite simple graphs, none of which is in Mathlib. ### Erdős' theorem on graphs of large girth and large chromatic number

[PALOMAR-2026-10-17-000009](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-17-000009&version=1)

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of Erdős' 1959 theorem: for every g and k there is a finite graph with girth greater than g and chromatic number greater than k. ### Moore graphs of diameter two: the Hoffman–Singleton theorem

### Chvátal's and Pósa's degree conditions for Hamiltonian cycles

[PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000001](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000001&version=1)

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of two classical degree-sequence conditions for Hamiltonicity of a finite simple graph on n ≥ 3 vertices with degrees d₁ ≤ ⋯ ≤ dₙ. Chvátal's theorem (1972): if for every i < n/2, dᵢ ≤ i implies d_{n−i} ≥ n − i, then the graph is Hamiltonian (stated without sorting: if at least i vertices have degree at most i, then at least i + 1 vertices have degree at least n − i). Pósa's theorem (1962): if for every k with 1 ≤ k < (n − 1)/2 fewer than k vertices have degree at most k, and, for odd n, at most (n − 1)/2 vertices have degree at most (n − 1)/2, then the graph is Hamiltonian. Mathlib has Hamiltonian cycles but neither theorem.

### The graph removal lemma for every fixed graph

[PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000002](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000002&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--graph-removal-lemma--03befbbe3fbf](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--graph-removal-lemma--03befbbe3fbf/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000002-v1/589cf03ef1e4c80ea8cdd6be77778232c62dd37c).

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of the graph removal lemma of Erdős, Frankl and Rödl for an arbitrary fixed finite graph H on k vertices: for every ε > 0 there is δ > 0, depending only on H and ε, such that every finite graph on n vertices with fewer than δ nᵏ labelled copies of H can be made H-free by deleting fewer than ε n² edges; equivalently (far form), a graph from which at least ε n² edges must be deleted to destroy all copies of H has at least δ nᵏ labelled copies. Also included is the counting lemma used in the proof: if parts s x (one for each vertex x of H) are robustly dense with density at least d along the edges of H, with 2kη ≤ dᵏ and #(s x) ≥ 4k/dᵏ, then at least (dᵏ/4)ᵏ ∏ #(s x) injective maps send H into G with f x ∈ s x. Mathlib has the regularity lemma and the triangle removal lemma, but not the removal lemma for a general H.

### Tutte's f-factor theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000005](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000005&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--tutte-f-factor--c791dc403ea0](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--tutte-f-factor--c791dc403ea0/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000005-v1/dc3bbce463c80c499d3e9fa072d13f9145d907d0).

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of Tutte's f-factor theorem: a finite simple graph G has a spanning subgraph H with deg_H v = f v for every vertex v if and only if, for all disjoint vertex sets S and T, q(S, T) ≤ ∑_{v ∈ S} f v − ∑_{v ∈ T} f v + ∑_{v ∈ T} deg_{G−S} v, where q(S, T) is the number of connected components C of G − (S ∪ T) for which ∑_{v ∈ C} f v + e(C, T) is odd and deg_{G−S} v is the number of neighbours of v outside S. Mathlib has Tutte's 1-factor theorem but not the f-factor theorem.

### Chordal, split, threshold and cograph structure

[PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000006](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000006&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--chordal-split-threshold-graphs--600d961f6749](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--chordal-split-threshold-graphs--600d961f6749/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000006-v1/ed65dcdc7339767d3e8e412420abbf54eae65ddb).

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of classical structure theorems for finite simple graphs. Chordal graphs: every minimal a–b separator is a clique, a nonempty finite chordal graph has a simplicial vertex, and a connected non-complete one has two non-adjacent simplicial vertices (Dirac 1961); a finite graph is chordal iff it has a perfect elimination order (Fulkerson–Gross 1965), iff it is the intersection graph of subtrees of a finite tree (Gavril 1974); a nonempty finite chordal graph has treewidth ω − 1, and every finite graph has ω ≤ tw + 1. Split graphs: split iff the graph and its complement are chordal, iff no induced 2K₂, C₄ or C₅ (Földes–Hammer 1977). Threshold graphs: threshold iff no induced 2K₂, C₄ or P₄ (Chvátal–Hammer 1977), iff split and a cograph (no induced P₄). Mathlib has none of these graph classes; their definitions are stated in the Challenge.

### Jaeger's 4-flow theorem

[PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000009](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000009&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--jaeger-four-flow--4443e53b8984](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--jaeger-four-flow--4443e53b8984/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000009-v1/f80cdd9128c8ce918978db640cc50623286e21fd).

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of Jaeger's 4-flow theorem: every finite 4-edge-connected simple graph has a nowhere-zero integer 4-flow, i.e. an antisymmetric function on darts with values in {±1, ±2, ±3} that is conserved at every vertex. Also proved: a graph with two edge-disjoint spanning trees has its edges covered by two even subgraphs (a nowhere-zero ℤ₂ × ℤ₂-flow) and has a nowhere-zero integer 4-flow. Mathlib has no flows. Jaeger's 8-flow theorem for bridgeless graphs is not included.

### Dirac's theorem: k vertices of a k-connected graph lie on a common cycle

[PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000011](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000011&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--dirac-common-cycle--7eeee5978914](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--dirac-common-cycle--7eeee5978914/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000011-v1/3c9e9966662cb99c0e4f11d771011bc904dc627f).

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of Dirac's 1960 theorem: in a k-connected finite simple graph with k ≥ 2 (more than k vertices, and connected after deleting any set of fewer than k vertices), any set of at most k vertices lies on a common cycle. In particular, in a 2-connected finite graph any two vertices lie on a common cycle. Cycles are Mathlib's `SimpleGraph.Walk.IsCycle`. Mathlib does not have this theorem.

### Spectral bounds on the chromatic and independence numbers

[PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000016](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000016&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--spectral-graph-bounds--08d3a675ea5f](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--spectral-graph-bounds--08d3a675ea5f/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000016-v1/8d8b4ce17e56165b5f1ad3b285fb141666804e1c).

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of classical eigenvalue bounds for a finite simple graph G on a nonempty vertex type, with λ_max and λ_min the largest and smallest eigenvalues of its real adjacency matrix: the average degree is at most λ_max and λ_max is at most the maximum degree (Collatz–Sinogowitz); G is (⌊λ_max⌋ + 1)-colourable (Wilf 1967); if G has an edge, every proper colouring uses at least 1 − λ_max/λ_min colours (Hoffman 1970); and if G is k-regular with k ≥ 1, every independent set has at most n(−λ_min)/(k − λ_min) vertices (Hoffman's ratio bound). Mathlib has the spectral theorem for Hermitian matrices but none of these bounds.

### Robbins' theorem on strongly connected orientations

[PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000022](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000022&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--robbins-strong-orientation--f05c2621ca9e](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--robbins-strong-orientation--f05c2621ca9e/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000022-v1/076e734e9949720d3e4fcc2cc3cad4284871442c).

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of Robbins' theorem: a finite connected simple graph has a strongly connected orientation (a digraph with underlying graph G, never containing both u → v and v → u, in which every vertex reaches every other) if and only if no edge of it is a bridge. As a corollary, every finite 2-edge-connected graph on a nonempty vertex type has a strongly connected orientation. Mathlib has bridges, edge connectivity and digraphs, but not this theorem.

### Galvin's theorem, the Dinitz problem and stable matchings

[PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000029](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000029&version=1)

Archived copy: [PalomarArchive/jtraverso--galvin-dinitz--befd03f21f2b](https://github.com/PalomarArchive/jtraverso--galvin-dinitz--befd03f21f2b/tree/palomar/PALOMAR-2026-10-18-000029-v1/0aa98fc17e1e96ec408f7bad3198ce37a08d26fc).

A Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of Galvin's theorem that the list chromatic index of a finite bipartite graph equals its maximum degree (if every edge has a list of at least Δ colours, a proper edge colouring can be chosen from the lists), of its corollary solving the Dinitz problem (an n × n array with lists of n symbols per cell admits a choice with distinct symbols in every row and column), and of the Gale–Shapley theorem on the existence of stable matchings with strict preferences and incomplete acceptability lists, which provides the kernels in Galvin's argument. Mathlib has line graphs and colourings but none of these results.
